    Robot vacuums shoppers are opening before Prime Big Deal Days¶

    Before you buy

    Specs below are copied from the Amazon listing, not from a test in this house. The dollar amounts are what Popular Science printed in its early-deals roundup. Check the live price before you order. These are affiliate links. See the disclosure.

    Robot vacuums are the smart-home product showing up in deal headlines on September 22, including NBC News and Popular Science. Two models those roundups name have listings that can be linked directly.

    Roborock Q7 M5¶

    Roborock Q7 M5, black

    The listing says:

    • 10,000 Pa suction
    • LiDAR navigation
    • Vacuum and mop in one pass, with app-controlled water flow
    • Dual anti-tangle brushes, aimed at pet hair
    • 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only

    Popular Science listed it at $149.98, down from $229.99. That figure is the roundup's price, not a promise that the tag page still shows it.

    The 2.4 GHz limit matters in the same houses as the lights that kept dropping offline. A phone on 5 GHz can still set it up, but the robot has to join the 2.4 GHz network.

    Shark AV2511AE¶

    Shark AV2511AE with self-empty base

    The listing title is Matrix Clean, model AV2511AE. Deal stories also call this line the AI Ultra. The listing says:

    • LiDAR home mapping
    • Bagless self-empty base with a stated 60-day capacity
    • Pet-hair pickup
    • Works with Alexa and Google Assistant
    • 120-minute battery in the listing's specification table

    Popular Science listed a Shark AI Ultra robot vacuum at $299.99, down from $599.00. Match the model number AV2511AE on the page before you treat that as this unit's price.

    What to check on the page¶

    • The price next to the button, not the number in a blog roundup
    • Whether the dock is in the box or sold as a separate station
    • Wi-Fi band, if the router hides 2.4 GHz under a single network name
    • The return window, because a robot that cannot map the floor is a return, not a settings tweak
